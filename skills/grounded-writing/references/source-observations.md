# Historical upstream writing references

This file records source observations used by the upstream version of the writing guidance. It is retained for provenance only. These sources are not the voice model for KotlinComposeKit, and their authors' experiences or opinions must not be attributed to SoufianoDev. The active editorial rules are defined in [style-profile.md](style-profile.md).

- [Shopping Is Not a Category](https://chrisbanes.me/posts/shopping-is-not-a-category/)
  (22 July 2026) opens with a personal budgeting problem, connects bank data to
  order emails, and shows an illustrative before/after table. It distinguishes
  what the automation can infer from what only Chris knows, then returns to the
  meaning of the original category. Its personal conclusion is bounded by the
  budgeting problem and the mechanism described in the post.
- [Haze 2.0](https://chrisbanes.me/posts/haze-2.0.0/)
  (22 September 2026) leads with what developers can build, explains the visible
  differences between Glass styles, then supplies API examples, measurements
  with their setup, experimental limits, and migration links. The announcement
  connects feature choices to visible behavior and usage examples.
- [The Disappearing Middle](https://chrisbanes.me/posts/disappearing-middle-ai-software-apprenticeship/)
  (29 December 2025) opens with a changed personal position, qualifies research,
  separates ways of working, and connects them to concrete risks and guardrails.
  The argument connects the author's experience and cited evidence to his
  practical judgment.
- [Should you use Kotlin Sequences for Performance?](https://chrisbanes.me/posts/use-sequence/)
  (13 February 2025) explains an assumption through familiar code, measures it,
  and candidly corrects the original expectation. Follow-up measurements extend
  the argument. The revised claim is tied to the measurements presented.
- [Retaining beyond ViewModels](https://chrisbanes.me/posts/retaining-beyond-viewmodels/)
  explains lifecycle differences through code and a navigation example, answers
  likely reader questions, and marks an experimental idea explicitly. Its asides
  and emoji are examples of Chris's playful tutorial register in that post.

The historical reference set consists of blog posts; it does not establish a
separate personal email corpus.
